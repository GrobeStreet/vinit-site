Straight with you

What's free here is genuinely free. VIN It reads the public U.S. government car databases (NHTSA) right on your phone, so there's nothing to charge for.

The one thing no app gives away free is a full accident and title history — that's Carfax and AutoCheck, and they charge for it. We don't pretend to provide it, and we're not affiliated with NHTSA or Carfax. We just make the free government records easy to read.

Where to find the VIN

1
On the car

Look through the windshield at the driver's-side dashboard, or open the driver's door and read the sticker in the jamb.

2
On paper

It's on your registration, your title, and your insurance card or app.

3
Easiest

Copy it from your insurance app and paste it straight in — no typing 17 characters.

Privacy Policy

Data Not Collected

VIN It is built so there's nothing to collect. Here is exactly how it handles information.

What leaves your phone

Only the VIN you choose to check. When you tap "Check this car," the app sends that VIN to the public U.S. government databases (the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, NHTSA) to look up the vehicle, the same way a web search works. Nothing else is sent.

What we don't do

  • No accounts, logins, names, emails or phone numbers.
  • No analytics, tracking, advertising or third-party SDKs.
  • No location, contacts, photos or device identifiers.
  • No selling or sharing of data — there is none to sell.

What stays on your phone

Your list of recent checks is stored on your device only, so you can tap back to a car you looked at. It never leaves the phone, and you can clear it any time from the home screen.

NHTSA

Vehicle data comes from NHTSA's free public APIs. Your use of their service is subject to NHTSA's own terms. VIN It is an independent app and is not affiliated with, or endorsed by, NHTSA.

Children

VIN It does not knowingly collect any information from anyone, including children.

Common questions

A VIN won't decode. Double-check all 17 characters — a VIN never uses the letters I, O or Q (those spots are ones and zeros). Very new or very old vehicles are sometimes missing from the government database.

Why no accident history? That isn't available free from anyone; see "Straight with you" above.
